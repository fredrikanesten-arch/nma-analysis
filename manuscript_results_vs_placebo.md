# Network Meta-Analysis: Treatment Effects vs. Placebo

*Manuscript-ready summaries of all non-placebo nodes versus placebo*

---

## 1. Acupuncture vs. Placebo

### Evidence base

This comparison is informed by 9 studies (k = 9), collectively enrolling 264 participants. This constitutes a modest evidence base within the network.

### Frequentist NMA estimate

The frequentist random-effects network estimate is SMD = -0.916 (95 CI: -1.442 to -0.391; z = -3.42, p = 0.0006), favouring the intervention.

### Bayesian estimate

The Bayesian random-effects posterior mean is SMD = 0.804 (95 CrI: 0.535 to 1.455); the 95 CrI does not include zero.

### Node-splitting consistency

This node is informed exclusively by indirect evidence; no direct head-to-head randomised trial versus placebo exists in the network. Node-splitting is therefore not applicable to this comparison.

### LOTO fragility

Removing all 9 studies contributing to this node simultaneously (leave-one-treatment-out, LOTO) produced a maximum absolute perturbation of Δ = 1.148 SMD units across all network estimates. The network is notably sensitive to the withdrawal of this treatment.

### LOSO fragility

Leave-one-study-out (LOSO) sensitivity analysis identified a maximum single-study perturbation of Δ = 0.535 SMD units on the network estimate for this treatment. One or more individual studies had moderate influence on the pooled result.

### Rank

Acupuncture achieved a SUCRA of 0.683, ranking 12 of 50 treatments in the network.

### Interpretation

Acupuncture demonstrates a statistically significant advantage over placebo in both frequentist and Bayesian analyses, with convergent credible and confidence intervals excluding the null. This constitutes consistent evidence of superiority over placebo.
